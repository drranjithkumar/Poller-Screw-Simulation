Poller Screw Simulator

Watch a straight intramedullary nail pass a proximal-third tibial fracture in two planes. Without blocking screws the wide metaphysis lets the fragment stay in valgus and procurvatum. A poller screw on the concave side narrows the canal, steers the nail, and forces the reduction as the nail enters the narrow diaphysis.

Placement rules the model demonstrates

  1. Concave side, short fragment. The screw goes in the metaphyseal (proximal) fragment, on the concave side of the deformity, so the nail is pushed toward the convexity.
  2. Proximal tibia. Valgus → lateral poller screw, inserted antero-posterior. Procurvatum (apex anterior) → posterior poller screw, inserted medio-lateral.
  3. Distance from the planned nail path ≈ nail radius + screw radius. Farther out leaves residual deformity; closer in over-corrects or blocks the nail.
  4. Timing. Place it about 1 cm from the fracture, before the guide wire and reaming. A Steinmann pin can mock up the position before the cortical screw goes in.
  5. Deforming forces. Patellar-tendon pull drives procurvatum. Semi-extended or suprapatellar nailing reduces it, and pollers hold what reduction achieves.

Teaching model in 2-D with rigid fragments: a straight nail (Herzog bend ignored), entry on the proximal fragment axis, 4.5 mm screws, and fracture 70 mm below the plateau. It shows direction and relative effect, not values for surgical planning. Further reading: Krettek C et al., JBJS Br 1999 (original description of Poller screws); PMC7144892; PMC7376586; AO Surgery Reference.
